# Research: The Expanding Unknown

Research pass: 18 October 2026. The brief preceded source retrieval. The seed conversation supplies the problem, not evidence. This is a selective conceptual review, not a systematic review or an original behavioral study.

## What changed during research

The seed's initial claim that the environment cannot cause curiosity is untenable as stated. An internally experienced motivation can be elicited by external encounters. The manuscript will distinguish elicitation, compulsion, behavior and reported experience.

More importantly, generativity is already present in the closest literature. Loewenstein (1994), pp. 89–90, explicitly allows new knowledge to alter the perceived information set. Patankar et al. (2023) model the growth and flexibility of knowledge networks. Liquin, Rhodes and Gureckis (2025) experimentally investigate reuse and recombination of questions. These are predecessors, not foils that assume a permanently fixed stock of gaps. The contribution must concern causal attribution and experimental discrimination, rather than priority for the idea that answers produce questions.

The 2024 conference paper on question reuse led to its fuller 2025 journal article. The journal version is the cited source; its weaker evidence for recombination under a grammar-based measure matters. Abir et al. (2026) offer a serious motivational alternative, but their satisfaction measures and information-prediction-error interpretation have acknowledged limits. Liquin (2026) supplies a particularly useful dissociation between reported curiosity and costly search. Its exception and measurement qualifications will remain visible.

## Source selection and reading

All thirteen retained references are tier 1 primary publications. Author-hosted manuscripts were used where publisher access was inconvenient. Public Europe PMC full-text XML was used for Liquin (2025; 2026) and Ten et al. (2021). Loewenstein's scanned author-hosted PDF required local OCR of pp. 87–90; no OCR text is reproduced as a quotation. Patankar's 2022 preprint was an initial lead; the 2023 publisher article was subsequently consulted and supplies the citation. Sources and supporting locations are recorded in sources.md.

The literature search followed four routes: information-gap/compression/learning-progress theories; question generation and knowledge networks; measures separating subjective curiosity from search behavior; and the seed's boundary cases of pedagogy, non-neural information use and pattern-guided exploration. Publisher records, author publications pages and the papers themselves resolved identities. Search summaries and the seed's bibliography were not accepted as sufficient support for detailed claims.

## Main evidential judgments

- Information-gap theory already allows a changing informational reference point. Opportunity generation can be a specification within that family of theories.
- Compression-progress and learning-progress models explain how informative activities become attractive. The proposed distinction asks separately which candidate activities the learner can formulate.
- Existing question-reuse experiments support a role for prior experience in later inquiry. They do not establish the proposed reusable-explanation effect, a universal rate of question production, or unchanged motivation.
- Increased information seeking alone cannot distinguish more candidates, lower costs, revised usefulness, changing motivation or task compliance. Self-report adds evidence without becoming a transparent measurement of an underlying drive.
- A conditional branching calculation is an existence argument. Its assumptions exclude convergence, fatigue and finite budgets; it is not a fitted model or a discovery of criticality in human curiosity.
- Chemotactic information use does not by itself demonstrate curiosity. Pattern-guided maze performance does not demonstrate the origin of motivation. Neither result licenses an inference about conscious experience.
- The toy-pedagogy experiments concern inferential restrictions in particular conditions. They cannot support an indictment of schooling as a whole.

## Decisions before drafting

Use a hypothetical repair problem, explicitly marked as such, to give the distinction a concrete setting. Keep the biological and artificial boundary cases short. Do not attempt a general theory of consciousness or assess Levin's metaphysics; the concrete Ertle–Levin–Scheutz study is sufficient for the cost-versus-motivation question.

No simulation is needed to establish the conditional expectation identity. No participant numbers, effect sizes or numerical model predictions will be invented. The prospective design must separate generating candidates from evaluating supplied candidates, explain the limits of a common-menu intervention, and state what results would count against the proposed mechanism. A null self-report difference will not be called equivalence.

## Remaining scientific work

The proposed human study has not been piloted, preregistered or conducted. The question-equivalence scheme requires reliability and construct-validity work. Generalization to classrooms, scientific communities and artificial agents remains open. Local verification will certify source accounting and the consistency of this manuscript's artifacts, not the empirical truth of the proposed account.
